## Why each tool is load-bearing

| Remove... | ...and this breaks |
|---|---|
| PipeGen | No perception at all. The backend has no inference endpoint, and the bridge only consumes PipeGen output. Nothing triggers the roadside unit except its own short-range sensor. |
| FirmGen firmware | Nothing acts. Species profiles, severity fusion, dedupe, alert extension, fail-safe and the ACK that measures latency all live on the board. |
| MQTT contract | The two halves cannot talk. It is written into the PRD as requirements WG-CON-03, WG-EVT-01..05 and WG-ACK-01. |

## Design decisions

| Decision | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Transport | MQTT over Wi-Fi, QoS 1 for events and ACKs | Named in the problem statement. PipeGen has an MQTT sink. Decoupled and works offline. |
| Pipeline lifecycle | Always running | Loading a TensorRT engine takes seconds. The sensor arms and confirms instead of waking the camera. |
| Sensor role | Second modality that upgrades severity | Two agreeing modalities mean fewer false alarms, which matters for a closed-set model. |
| Who decides the response | The ESP32 | Keeps FirmGen load-bearing and keeps the roadside unit safe when the laptop link drops. |
| Latency measurement | ACK round trip on one clock (laptop) plus on-board RX-to-ACT from the ESP32 uptime clock | No clock sync needed on the ESP32. |
| Backend role | Observer and controller, not in the critical path | The loop works with the backend stopped. |

## Real-world framing

The deployed unit's main job is to **warn drivers** with a flashing roadside sign before an animal crossing. The acoustic deterrent is secondary. The bench rig uses an HC-SR04 with roughly 4 m range as a stand-in for a long-range sensor (radar, LiDAR or a PIR array). Future work includes solar power, LoRa for roads without Wi-Fi, multi-camera coverage, and retraining with background and negative images.
